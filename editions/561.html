Your Daily Artificial Intelligence Gazette

AI Daily Briefing — July 28, 2026

Today's digest is headlined by a surge of infrastructure anxiety — from grid operators threatening to cut power to data centers, to chip stocks selling off amid AI valuation jitters. Meanwhile, developers get meaningful SDK fixes, Anthropic drops cryptography research, and the agentic AI wave keeps crashing into new shores.

Infrastructure & Energy

Data centers may face temporary power cuts to prevent blackouts on largest US grid — The breakneck pace of data center construction is now a legitimate grid stability threat. PJM, which operates the largest US power grid, is weighing emergency curtailment measures that would temporarily shut off power to data centers during peak demand periods. This is the clearest sign yet that AI infrastructure appetite is outpacing energy supply planning.

Chip stocks tumble as AI sell-off deepens — Semiconductor equities took a notable hit today as investor sentiment around AI valuations continued to sour. The sell-off reflects growing scrutiny of when — and whether — the massive capital being poured into AI infrastructure will translate into commensurate returns.

Funding & Industry Moves

Recursive Superintelligence signs $410M compute deal with Amazon — The self-improving AI startup is routing virtually all of its war chest directly into AWS compute, betting that automation can replace the headcount and operational overhead that traditional AI labs carry. It's a provocative organizational thesis: what if the AI itself can do most of the work of building the AI?

Fish Audio raises $52M seed to build AI voice models for creators and enterprises — One of the larger seed rounds in recent memory, Fish Audio is capitalizing on 8 million users and $21M ARR to expand its open-source and hosted voice model offerings. The raise signals continued enterprise appetite for controllable, creator-friendly voice AI that doesn't route everything through a single incumbent API.

Agentic AI & Assistants

Perplexity's Personal Computer turns Windows PCs into AI agents — Perplexity's locally-run agentic computer-use tool is now on Windows, dramatically expanding its addressable market beyond Mac users. The feature lets the AI operate the desktop directly, positioning Perplexity squarely against Microsoft's own Copilot ambitions on the world's most popular OS — on Microsoft's own turf.

Scientific computing in the age of agentic AI — OpenAI makes the case that agentic AI systems are ready to take on serious scientific computing workloads — not just code generation, but end-to-end research pipelines. It's a vision statement as much as a technical report, and it pairs interestingly with Anthropic's cryptography research (see below) as labs race to demonstrate frontier scientific utility.

Research & Security

Discovering Cryptographic Weaknesses with Claude — Anthropic's research team demonstrates Claude autonomously identifying cryptographic vulnerabilities — a meaningful benchmark for AI-assisted security research. This kind of work is a double-edged sword: it validates AI as a serious offensive/defensive security tool while raising questions about responsible deployment boundaries.

Fast Remediation Is the New Trust Model (JFrog and OpenAI Zero-Day Findings) — JFrog and OpenAI detail a coordinated zero-day disclosure process, arguing that remediation speed — not just disclosure policies — should define how we measure trust in software supply chains. Timely for an industry increasingly reliant on AI-generated and AI-audited code.

"Uncensored" open LLMs are measurably more optimistic than their base models — A preprint finds that fine-tuning LLMs to remove safety guardrails doesn't just change what they'll say — it measurably shifts their expressed sentiment toward optimism. The finding complicates narratives that "uncensored" models are simply more neutral; they appear to have their own systematic biases introduced by the fine-tuning process.

Open Source & Developer Tools

Coding Tools MCP (v0.2.2): Give any AI chat or agent a pair of hands on your code — A community MCP server that exposes code editing primitives to any MCP-compatible AI client, letting chat interfaces and agents read, write, and modify files without being locked into a specific IDE integration. A practical building block for teams rolling their own agentic coding workflows.

Now Is the Time to Give LLMs Access to the ACM Digital Library — An ACM opinion piece argues that locking LLMs out of the ACM's corpus of peer-reviewed CS research is an own goal for the field. The argument: if AI is going to do serious scientific and engineering work, it needs access to the canonical literature — and the ACM is well-positioned to lead with a thoughtful licensing model.

Show HN: Segue – Save context in one AI, load it in another by a short handle — A clever developer tool that lets you snapshot conversation context and reload it in a different AI client via a short handle. Useful for anyone juggling multiple models or switching between Claude, GPT, and others mid-workflow without losing thread.

Claude Code Developer Corner

Two Python SDK releases landed today in quick succession — both focused on MCP compatibility, which is increasingly the connective tissue of the Claude tooling ecosystem.

anthropic-sdk-python v0.120.1 pinned the MCP extra dependency to <2, a defensive fix to prevent breakage as the MCP SDK ecosystem transitions to a major version bump. v0.120.2 followed hours later with the real fix: full support for MCP SDK v2 alongside v1, so developers are no longer forced to choose between them.

What this means practically: If you're running MCP-enabled Python workflows against the Anthropic SDK, you should upgrade to v0.120.2 immediately. The v1 pin in v0.120.1 was a short-lived stopgap; v0.120.2 is the correct release to be on. Developers building MCP servers or clients that need to support both SDK generations — common in enterprise environments where not everything upgrades in lockstep — now have a supported path without forking or patching.

Migration note: No breaking changes are introduced, but if you manually pinned mcp<2 as a workaround in your own requirements.txt, you can remove that constraint after upgrading to v0.120.2.

Worth Watching

Formally verified 3D CSG in Lean 4 — A developer claims this is the first formally verified 3D constructive solid geometry implementation, written in Lean 4. The framing is pointed: trust 93 lines of specification, not 1,000 lines of AI-generated code. A sharp commentary on verification vs. generation, and a genuinely interesting piece of applied formal methods work.

Cynative – Read-only CLI that explains your live infrastructure — An open-source Go CLI that connects to GitHub, GitLab, AWS, GCP, Azure, and Kubernetes using existing credentials to answer security posture questions. Read-only by design, making it safer to run in production environments. One to watch for platform and security engineers.

DMARC Has Been Public Since 2012. 68.4% of Domains Still Don't Enforce It — A useful data point as AI-generated phishing scales: the email authentication infrastructure that would help defend against spoofing is still wildly underdeployed, 14 years on. Relevant context for anyone building AI-assisted security tooling.
